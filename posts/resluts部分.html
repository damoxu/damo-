latex\section{Experiments and Results}\label{sec:experiments}
\subsection{Experimental Setup}

\subsubsection{Datasets}
We constructed a private porcine CT dataset comprising three components. 
(1)~\textbf{Unlabeled pretraining data}: 3,084 CT scans collected from multiple 
farms using an agricultural-grade CT scanner, with heads and forelimbs removed 
prior to acquisition. No manual annotation was required.
(2)~\textbf{Primary dataset (Dataset~A)}: 104 pigs from four breeds (Yorkshire, 
Duroc, Landrace, and Pietrain). For each subject, CT slices were acquired from 
the ear margin posteriorly and annotated across nine anatomical regions: anterior 
body (ear margin to ribs 6--7), middle body (ribs 6--7 to caudal vertebrae 1--2), 
posterior body (caudal vertebrae 1--2 onward), thoracic cavity, abdominal cavity 
(separated at the stomach), left kidney, right kidney, and testes. Data were split 
at the subject level: 72 pigs for training, 8 for validation, and 24 as a held-out 
in-domain test set.
(3)~\textbf{Cross-domain dataset (Dataset~B)}: 93 castrated Yorkshire pigs from a 
different farm scanned with a different CT device, annotated following the same 
protocol as Dataset~A. This set serves as an out-of-domain test set to assess 
cross-device and cross-breed generalization.

\subsubsection{Implementation Details}
\label{sec:implementation}
All experiments were implemented in PyTorch with the MONAI framework and trained 
on a single NVIDIA A100 (40\,GB) GPU with automatic mixed precision (AMP).

\paragraph{Pre-training.}
The backbone is SwinUNETR~\citep{liu2021swin} with feature size 48. Input volumes 
were clipped to $[-175, 250]$\,HU, normalized to $[0,1]$, resampled to 
$1.5\times1.5\times1.5$\,mm$^3$, and cropped to $96\times96\times96$ voxels. 
Training ran for 25,000 steps (batch size 2) using AdamW 
($\text{lr}=1\times10^{-4}$, weight decay $0.1$) with a cosine schedule and 
5,000 warmup steps.

\paragraph{Fine-tuning.}
The pretrained encoder was initialized and fine-tuned end-to-end for 500 epochs 
(batch size 2) using AdamW ($\text{lr}=5\times10^{-4}$, weight decay $0.05$) 
with a cosine schedule. Online augmentation included random rotations 
($\pm10^\circ$), flips, and scaling ($0.9$--$1.1$). Early stopping patience was 
set to 20 epochs.

\paragraph{Evaluation.}
Segmentation performance was measured by the Dice Similarity Coefficient (DSC). 
To ensure statistical reliability, each method was evaluated under six random seeds 
(0, 21, 42, 123, 456, 789) and results are reported as mean\,$\pm$\,std.

\subsection{\HKY Consistently Outperforms All Pre-training Strategies}
\label{subsec:sota}

We compare \HKY against four baselines: (1)~training from scratch 
(w/o pre-training), (2)~SwinUNETR SSL~\citep{hatamizadeh2021swin}, 
(3)~VOCO~\citep{wu2024voco}, and (4)~the jigsaw pretext task without human 
prior initialization (\ours). All methods share the same SwinUNETR backbone.

As shown in Tables~\ref{tab:sota_trainval} and~\ref{tab:sota_extended}, \HKY 
achieves the best performance on both validation and test sets across all six 
random seeds. On the validation set, \HKY attains a mean DSC of 
$95.02\pm0.97\%$, surpassing the next best method (\ours, $86.79\pm3.93\%$) by 
8.23 percentage points. On the internal test set, \HKY achieves $93.37\pm1.62\%$, 
outperforming \ours ($85.08\pm2.56\%$) by 10.07 points. Notably, \HKY also 
exhibits the lowest standard deviation among all methods, indicating substantially 
improved training stability and reduced sensitivity to random initialization. 
These results confirm that the combination of cross-species transfer initialization 
and the 3D jigsaw pretext task provides complementary and additive benefits.

\subsection{Data Efficiency: \HKY with 50\% Data Surpasses All Baselines 
            with Full Data}

To assess label efficiency, we trained all models on 25\%, 50\%, 75\%, and 100\% 
of the training set (Table~\ref{tab:datapercent}). \HKY demonstrates exceptional 
data efficiency: with only 50\% of labeled data it achieves 93.76\% DSC, 
exceeding the best competing method trained on the full dataset (\ours at 86.31\%) 
by 7.45 percentage points. Even at 25\% data, \HKY (79.95\%) outperforms all 
baselines at 100\% data. In contrast, all other methods exhibit relatively flat 
scaling behavior, suggesting they are unable to effectively leverage the limited 
labeled samples without strong pretraining. These results demonstrate that 
cross-species transfer initialization substantially reduces the annotation burden 
required for high-performance pig CT segmentation.

\subsection{Organ-level Analysis: Largest Gains on Kidney Segmentation}

Using seed 42 on the 24-sample internal test set, we analyzed per-organ 
performance via box plots (Figure~\ref{fig:organ}). Across all six organ 
categories (anterior, middle, posterior, testicle, thoracic, abdominal), \HKY 
achieves the highest median DSC, followed by \ours, with SwinUNETR, VOCO, and 
the no-pretraining baseline ranking lower. \HKY also exhibits the smallest 
interquartile range in most organs, confirming consistent segmentation quality.

The most pronounced gains are observed for the kidneys 
(Figure~\ref{fig:organ}b). SwinUNETR and VOCO show notably poor right-kidney 
segmentation, while \ours already improves over the baseline. \HKY further 
substantially improves both left and right kidney DSC, with the smallest 
interquartile range among all methods, suggesting that cross-species anatomical 
priors are particularly beneficial for small, morphologically consistent structures 
whose topology is well-preserved across human and pig anatomy.

\subsection{Cross-Domain Generalization to Unseen Scanner and Breed}

We evaluated all models on Dataset~B (93 Large White pigs, different scanner). 
As shown in Figure~\ref{fig:animal_orgen}, \HKY retains clear advantages over 
competing methods in the middle body, thoracic, and abdominal regions. The right 
kidney remains a failure case for SwinUNETR under this domain shift, while 
\HKY maintains substantially higher accuracy.

A notable performance drop is observed in the anterior and posterior regions 
across all self-supervised methods. We attribute this to a systematic training 
distribution mismatch: the training set consists of carcasses with heads and 
hooves removed, whereas Dataset~B includes intact animals. This causes the model 
to mislocalize the anteriorly positioned hooves as the posterior region and the 
cranial volume as anterior/posterior segments. Despite this domain gap, \HKY 
maintains disproportionately high kidney segmentation accuracy, consistent with 
the organ's topological stability across domains.

\subsection{Breed-level Generalization Across Four Pig Breeds}

We stratified the 24-test-set results by breed (6 pigs each: Yorkshire, Duroc, 
Landrace, Pietrain). As shown in Figure~\ref{fig:breeds}, \HKY achieves the 
highest overall DSC across all breeds, with \ours ranking second. SwinUNETR and 
VOCO underperform relative to even the no-pretraining baseline on several breeds, 
suggesting that standard pretraining strategies without anatomical priors may 
introduce representations that conflict with breed-specific morphological variation. 
Yorkshire and Pietrain exhibit relatively higher inter-individual variance, 
reflecting their broader phenotypic diversity.

\subsection{Ablation: Finer Jigsaw Granularity Improves Segmentation}

We investigated the effect of jigsaw patch size by evaluating three crop scales 
from volumes resized to $384\times384\times96$: full size (1/1), one-quarter 
(1/4), and one-eighth (1/8). As shown in Figure~\ref{fig:The_size_of_the_block}, 
finer granularity consistently yields higher segmentation DSC. We attribute this 
to the fact that smaller patches force the encoder to capture finer-grained local 
appearance cues alongside the global spatial context required for correct 
position prediction, producing richer multi-scale representations for downstream 
segmentation.

\subsection{Qualitative Segmentation Results}

Figure~\ref{fig:Visualization} shows segmentation outputs from three anatomical 
views (axial, coronal, sagittal) for all five methods. \HKY produces the most 
complete and anatomically consistent delineations, with notably sharper boundaries 
for the left and right kidneys. Other methods, particularly SwinUNETR and VOCO, 
exhibit fragmented kidney contours and elevated false-positive regions. \HKY also 
generates fewer spurious predictions in surrounding tissues, indicating that the 
learned representations are more specific to true anatomical boundaries.
